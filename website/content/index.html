Definograph

Definograph draws the structure of mathematical statements written in Lean. See the order of choices, which variables may depend on which, the assumptions and requirements, and the definitions behind them. It reads the statement as Lean has understood it, with its notation resolved and every type filled in, not as printed text.

Definograph is a research prototype that runs on your own computer. It does not prove statements or say whether they are true. The figures on this site are recorded output from it, and the site itself runs no Lean, so you can read everything here without installing anything. To run Definograph yourself, see Try it yourself.

The app window

You type a statement into the Lean expression field of Definograph’s Lean source panel, or choose one from its Statement menu, and Lean reads it. Definograph first shows the statement’s Visual sequence: a step-by-step reading, one choice, assumption or condition at a time, with an outline of the whole statement beside it. The Explore a sample tab holds the other views as tabs of their own: Choices, Relationships and Structure, and for some statements numerical views such as Condition, which varies one variable while the other choices stay fixed. The Mathematical notation button shows the statement typeset. In Visual Studio Code, Definograph reads a selection in your own Lean project in the same way.

What the views show

Each view answers one reading question. Lean checks that the statement is well formed; the views draw its structure.

The order of choices

Which choice may depend on which? The Choices view above lists what the statement introduces in reading order and, under each candidate witness, the earlier choices it may use. More in {{lesson:01-order-of-choices.md}}.

Objects and relations

Which relations does an object take part in? The Structure view shows the displayed objects and relations in one overview. Here x is selected in g (f x) = h x, so its connections are drawn heavier than the rest.

{{view:paths-trace-x}}

Where does the same object appear? In the Visual sequence, selecting x draws a dashed thread down the page, joining each place where that x occurs, in the current step and in the full visual statement below it. Its route means nothing; it only links the places. More in {{lesson:02-objects-and-relations.md}}.

Specialized figures

What does a condition require? For notions that Definograph supports, such as sets, graph colorings, balls in a metric space and maps that are inverse on a region, the Visual sequence adds a figure. For a proper coloring, the figure shows the rule on every edge: adjacent endpoints get different labels.

In the Lean editor

In Visual Studio Code, Definograph also records what Lean checked for a selection in your project, including which axioms a proof relies on; {{lesson:06-checking-evidence.md}} shows how to read that record.

Status

Definograph is not a general mathematical visualizer. It runs beside Lean 4.28.0 on your own computer. Its source is available in the Definograph GitHub repository; Try it yourself explains the build and setup requirements.

The formal foundations already prove general results about specified encodings of Lean expressions and local source records: decoding an encoding recovers the expression’s structure, and the encoded forms can be printed as text and read back exactly. These results say what can be recovered from an encoding under its stated conditions; the Reference explains what they cover.

The goal is a formally proved general visualization method for any valid Lean mathematical statement. The current application provides bounded structural inspection and selected guided readings; a universal guarantee connecting valid inputs to the rendered interactive view remains unproved. Mathematics that Definograph does not interpret stays visible as typed or logical structure.
