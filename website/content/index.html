Definograph

Definograph draws the structure of mathematical statements written in Lean: the order of choices, which variables may depend on which, the assumptions and requirements, and the definitions behind them. It reads the statement as Lean has understood it, with its notation resolved and every type filled in, not as printed text. Definograph is a research prototype that runs on your own computer. It does not prove statements or say whether they are true. The figures on this site are recorded output from it, and the site itself runs no Lean, so you can read everything here without installing anything.

Two views of two statements. The Choices view reads ∀ x : ℝ, ∃ y : ℝ, x < y from the top: x is chosen first, and y may depend on it. The Relationships view reads A ⊆ B → x ∈ A → x ∈ B relation by relation, with each object in its role and a note of where the relation sits.

Status

Definograph is not a general mathematical visualizer. It runs beside Lean 4.28.0 on your own computer. Its source is available in the Definograph GitHub repository; Try it yourself explains the build and setup requirements.

The formal foundations already prove general results about specified encodings of Lean expressions and local source records: decoding an encoding recovers the expression’s structure, and the encoded forms can be printed as text and read back exactly. These results say what can be recovered from an encoding under its stated conditions; the Reference explains what they cover.

The goal is a formally proved general visualization method for any valid Lean mathematical statement. The current application provides bounded structural inspection and selected guided readings; a universal guarantee connecting valid inputs to the rendered interactive view remains unproved. Mathematics that Definograph does not interpret stays visible as typed or logical structure.
